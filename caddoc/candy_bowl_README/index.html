    Candy Bowl — Parametric Decorative Vessel in CadQuery¶

    A fully parametric decorative bowl modelled entirely in code using CadQuery. The geometry, surface patterns, and ornamental cuts are all derived analytically — no mesh sculpting, no GUI modelling tools.

    Overview¶

    This script builds a decorative candy bowl with a spherical body, a scalloped rim, and five distinct ornamental patterns cut into the surface. Every geometric decision — the wave profile of the rim, the diamond facets, the helical grooves — is expressed as a mathematical formula and applied through CadQuery's solid modelling kernel (OCCT).

    The result is exported as a GLB file ready for 3D printing or rendering.

    Construction pipeline¶

    1. Base body¶

    The bowl starts as a unit sphere, clipped by two horizontal cutting planes to produce an open hemispherical cup with a flat bottom. A shell operation (shell(-w)) hollows the body to a uniform wall thickness w = 0.09.

    2. Scalloped rim — fn_wave()¶

    The rim is not a plain circle. A sinusoidal wave function

    z = d · sin(α · n)

    is sampled around the perimeter to generate a closed spline with n = 6 lobes. A circular profile is lofted along this path and subtracted from the shell, leaving a rhythmically scalloped upper edge. The amplitude d = 0.15 and lobe count are fully parametric.

    3. Surface patterns¶

    Five independent ornamental patterns are cut into the sphere surface as Boolean subtractions.

    pattern0 — flat diamond ring¶

    A single lens-shaped groove (diamond_disc) is drawn between two points on a horizontal circle, then rotated nn = 19 times around the vertical axis to form a continuous decorative band near the base. The groove cross-section is the intersection of two spheres, giving a smooth concave profile.

    pattern1 — polar cap cluster¶

    Nine diamond_disc grooves are arranged on a small polar cap near the top of the bowl, forming a star-like incised rosette. The whole cluster is rotated to align with the rim lobes and then repeated n2 = 6 times around the axis.

    pattern2 — skew-chord ring¶

    Similar to pattern0 but placed on a tighter latitude circle with a skip of ns = 5 steps between chord endpoints, producing a star polygon outline. The assembly is tilted and repeated to interleave with pattern1.

    pattern3 — helical diamond grooves¶

    diamond_line() sweeps a square cross-section along a parametric helix whose radial offset oscillates with a cubic ease-out term near the bottom. Two mirror-image helices (clockwise and counter-clockwise) are cut simultaneously, producing a crossed lattice of raised diamond ridges running from the base up the body.

    d = (t - 0.5)*2
d = d*d*deep/2 + k*(1-t)**3 * deep

    The cubic term k·(1−t)³ gently flares the groove outward at the base so the ridges fade smoothly into the flat bottom zone rather than terminating abruptly.

    pattern4 — latitude ring stack¶

    Seven horizontal ring grooves are cut at decreasing latitudes near the top, each with slightly decreasing depth and angular width. Together they form a series of concentric incised rings that frame the scalloped rim.

    Key techniques¶

    diamond_disc(a, b, norm, d, alf) — the core primitive. Given two endpoints and a surface normal, it constructs a lens groove as the intersection of two offset spheres. The depth ratio d and opening angle alf control the sharpness of the cut. Used by pattern0, pattern1, and pattern2.

    diamond_line(sol, h0, h1, alf_shift, deep) — sweeps a diamond-section profile along a parametric helix, using ray-casting (sol.intersect(line)) to pin each point to the actual surface of the solid. This means the groove follows the sphere's curvature exactly regardless of the helix parameters.

    multi_disc() / multi_disc_comp() — rotational repetition helpers. multi_disc fuses copies into a single solid for Boolean cutting; multi_disc_comp returns a Compound for lighter preview. The distinction matters for performance with many copies.

    Design notes¶

    All five surface patterns share a single geometric primitive — the lens-shaped diamond_disc — but vary in placement, orientation, and repetition count to produce visually distinct decorations. Keeping one reusable primitive makes it straightforward to add new pattern bands or change symmetry without rewriting the cutting logic.

    The helical grooves in pattern3 use live ray-casting against the current solid to position each path point. This couples the groove geometry to the bowl's actual surface, so the pattern automatically adapts if the sphere radius or clip heights are changed.
